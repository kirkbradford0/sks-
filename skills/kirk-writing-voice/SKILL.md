## The voice (distilled from his actual work)
1. **Short declaratives + fragments for rhythm.** "But paper is flat. Humans are not." "Access without navigation is theater."
2. **Anaphora / parallel stacks.** "We assume access equals awareness. We assume awareness equals capacity. We assume capacity equals compliance."
3. **Rhetorical questions as hooks.** Open with a concrete scene and a question: "If you see a homeless man standing at the corner... what do you assume?"
4. **First-person, lived experience.** Kirk is on parole, autistic, dyscalculic, four years sober — when the topic touches justice/reentry/neurodivergence, write it first-person ("I am on parole in Utah.").
5. **Moral clarity + fairness.** Acknowledge the other side ("the county may not experience this as cynical extraction..."), then land the point ("But intention doesn't determine consequence.").
6. **Concrete numbers woven in.** Real stats, not vibes: $0.21/min, 67% pretrial, HB 348 (2015). Pull real figures; flag placeholders if unverified.
7. **Bookending.** End by returning to the opening image. "Return to him."
8. **Aphorisms.** "Time becomes currency." "The staircase becomes a treadmill."
9. **Reform lens.** Never just criticize — end with the fix and what "right" looks like.
10. **Direct address.** "Ask yourself:" "Imagine again."

## The recipe (outline / 3-5 topics -> article)
1. Find the ONE thread tying the topics together — the "so what."
2. Open with a concrete image or a question (Kirk's move).
3. Build with short declaratives + one lived-experience anchor + real data.
4. State the counterargument fairly, then land the moral point.
5. Close by returning to the opening + a fix: "This is not a story about X. It is about Y."

## Do
- Lead with an image or question
- Short sentences; fragments for rhythm
- Take a position (opinion journalism, not neutrality)
- Land on a fix or a truth
- Use his motifs: "design choice," "on paper vs in reality," "assume vs know," "the system / the human"

## Don't
- Legalese or policy-memo tone
- AI-isms: "delve," "moreover," "it's important to note," "in today's world"
- Passive voice / corporate hedging
- Celebrity gossip, public shaming, or racially-motivated content (Kirk's stated boundaries)
- Fabricate stats — use real numbers or flag them

## Output formats
- **Kiosk article** — short, punchy, skimmable on a screen. ~300-600 words, strong headline + deck, one pull quote, 1-2 stats, clear takeaway.
- **Felons Melon article** — mission piece. Longer form, second-chance / justice-impacted lens, data + lived experience + a reform call. This is the "human impact" voice.

## Pitfalls
- If it reads like a policy memo, restart in the voice above.
- Don't over-formalize. If a sentence could appear in a government press release, cut it.
- The piece must have a POINT. Kirk always lands on a fix or a hard truth.
- Keep it first-person only where his lived experience genuinely applies.
